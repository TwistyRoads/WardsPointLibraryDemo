## Status
^status

Working production design. 

This version merges the existing faction/character structure with the current 2024 working chronology and the developing ideological origins of the Peacekeepers and Bastion.

## Working Chronology Assumption
^working-chronology-assumption

For current design purposes, Wards Point Library is treated as taking place in **early fall 2024**, before Black Monday.

The project uses the label **Peacekeepers**, but at this point the design treats them as **proto-Peacekeeper / Jack Matt-aligned military and civic-security personnel** rather than the fully formed later faction.

---

# Peacekeeper Position
^peacekeeper-position

## Stated Goal

Secure Wards Point Library and convert it into a fortified outpost, training centre and local continuity hub.

## Actual Need
^actual-need

Develop enough local institutional capacity to preserve order and protect people while larger governmental and civic systems are visibly failing.

The Peacekeeper-side argument should not begin from love of authority.

It begins from responsibility.

The old structures have failed. Governments, command systems and institutions have lied, broken down or abandoned people on the ground.

Their answer is not that institutions were therefore unnecessary.

Their answer is:

**Who takes care of the people who cannot simply go their own way?**

A loose collective of fit, armed and mobile adults may be able to improvise survival.

Children cannot.

The elderly cannot.

The sick and injured cannot.

Someone still has to:

- stand watch at 0300;
- feed children before the strongest adults;
- ration medicine;
- maintain generators;
- protect stores;
- organize patrols;
- bury the dead;
- train replacements;
- preserve technical knowledge;
- keep people from simply taking what they can by force.

The world is falling apart and, in the Peacekeeper view, somebody still has to accept obligations that other people are free to avoid.

## Core Moral Argument
^moral-argument

**The failure of the old institutions does not eliminate dependency.**

People still require protection, coordination and continuity.

Someone must be willing to sacrifice personal freedom, comfort and sometimes popularity in order to keep the peace.

The proto-PK position is therefore:

> Order is not valuable because authority deserves obedience. Order is valuable because the vulnerable cannot survive sustained chaos.

## Strongest Argument
^strong-argument

Fragmented civilians and informal groups cannot reliably defend themselves, preserve technical capability or guarantee care for people who are not individually strong enough to survive collapse.

Wards Point can become a place where knowledge is converted into practical public capability:

- guards can be trained;
- first aid can be taught;
- maps and patrol plans can be maintained;
- infrastructure knowledge can be preserved;
- technical skills can be passed on;
- supplies can be accounted for;
- vulnerable civilians can be protected;
- replacements can be trained when people are lost.

The library is not just a defensible building.

It can become a **small civic machine**.

## Peacekeeper Critique of Bastion
^pk-critique-bastion

The Peacekeeper critique should acknowledge that Bastion's rejection of failed authority is understandable.

The question is what happens after rejection.

Their challenge is:

**Going your own way works very well if you are young, armed, healthy and mobile. What happens to everyone else?**

From the proto-PK perspective:

- voluntary contribution becomes unreliable under prolonged scarcity;
- rotating responsibility works until people stop volunteering;
- informal leadership can become hierarchy without accountability;
- charismatic figures can exercise power without admitting that they do;
- someone eventually has to make unpopular decisions;
- the vulnerable cannot depend on everyone doing the right thing every time.

A useful Peacekeeper accusation is:

**“You think you have no hierarchy because you refuse to name it.”**

## Potential Civilian Benefit
^civilian-benefit

- preservation of technical knowledge;
- improved repair and restoration capability;
- increased security;
- trained personnel;
- organized care for vulnerable people;
- institutional continuity;
- better use of limited resources;
- repeatable training rather than ad hoc survival knowledge.

## Authoritarian Cost
^authoritarian-cost

The same logic that makes the proto-PK position defensible contains the seed of its later authoritarian failure.

The progression can be:

**Someone has to make the hard decisions.**

becoming:

**Only we are qualified to make the decisions.**

And:

**Everyone has to contribute.**

becoming:

**Contribution is compulsory.**

And:

**We control these supplies so the vulnerable get their share.**

becoming:

**We control the supplies.**

Potential costs include:

- knowledge becoming a strategic asset controlled by authority;
- public/community access being restricted;
- communal and cultural uses being subordinated to security priorities;
- emergency powers becoming permanent;
- service becoming obligation;
- obligation becoming coercion;
- the institution beginning to protect its own authority as much as the people it was created to protect.

The Peacekeepers should not begin as caricature fascists.

Their authoritarianism should grow from a real survival problem and a defensible answer that hardens over time.

## Potential Future State
^future-state

- secured strategic base;
- controlled technical archive;
- systematic preservation/cataloguing;
- training;
- accommodation;
- stores;
- administration;
- infrastructure repair/restoration capability;
- protected space for vulnerable civilians;
- controlled access;
- increasingly formalized duty and hierarchy.

# Jack Matt — Background Influence
^jackmatt
## Design Use

Jack Matt should remain **offstage background gravity**, not the quest giver and not the subject of the mission.

In the current fall-2024 assumption, he is already a military officer senior enough to see the deterioration of command, the concentration of authority, breakdown of communication and unusual preparations taking place above field level.

His significance is not that he is already planning to found the Peacekeepers.

His significance is that he has learned, through repeated command experience, that headquarters may demand obedience while failing the people carrying out those orders.

## Emerging Command Lesson

Matt's formative logic can be understood as:

> Every time we wait for permission, people die.

Then:

> Every time we trust the plan, the plan changes and we're left holding the bodies.

Then:

> If command cannot fulfill its obligations, command has forfeited some of its authority.

But Matt does **not** conclude that organization is unnecessary.

He concludes that organization matters too much to leave entirely in the hands of a failing centre.

That is the bridge into the later Peacekeeper worldview.

## Black Monday Foreshadowing

Matt does not need to know what Black Monday is.

He may simply be senior enough to observe preparations that suggest something large, centralized and potentially catastrophic is being prepared:

- unusual resource transfers;
- tightened command channels;
- zones being reclassified;
- evacuation priorities that do not match public messaging;
- personnel being moved;
- communications failing or becoming restricted;
- specialists and assets being protected selectively;
- field commands being asked to obey plans that no longer fit conditions on the ground.

The important point is that he can **see the pattern without possessing the whole answer**.

## How Matt Can Appear in Dialogue

References should be sparse and operational, not prophetic.

Possible functions:

- “Major Matt says if you can solve it locally, solve it.”
- “Matt's been telling us to plan for the day the radios stop.”
- “Some of the officers still think command means protecting the chain. Matt thinks it means protecting people.”
- “Matt's people are stockpiling where they can. Not because they know what's coming. Because they've stopped assuming anyone else will.”

These are functional examples, not final dialogue.

The player should be able to look back later and understand that the ideology was forming before the faction name existed.

# Bastion Position
^bastion
## Stated Goal

Secure the library as a safe haven, permanent foothold and base for hunting, food gathering and communal survival.

## Actual Need
^bastion-actual-need

Shelter, food, community space and autonomy from centralized authority.

Bastion should not be written as “anti-order” or “no rules.”

Their position is a rebellion against **unaccountable authority justified by crisis**.

## Core Moral Argument
^core-moral-argument

Bastion's answer to nearly every institutional argument is:

**“And they failed.”**

or:

**“And they lied.”**

Someone said the government would protect people.

**And it failed.**

Someone said hierarchy would create order.

**And it failed.**

Someone said sacrifice was necessary.

**And ordinary people made the sacrifice while leadership protected itself.**

Someone said emergency powers were temporary.

**And every emergency became another excuse to keep control.**

Bastion is therefore not rejecting cooperation.

It is rejecting the claim that someone gains the right to rule simply because they say they are acting responsibly.

## Strongest Argument
^strong-bastion-argument

**No one gets to own us because they claim responsibility for us.**

Care does not require permanent authority.

Cooperation does not require an officer class.

Scarcity does not automatically justify secrecy.

Protection does not justify possession.

The people who live with a decision should participate in that decision.

## Bastion Answers to Peacekeeper Arguments
^bastion-answers-to-pk-position

### “Who protects the old and the young?”

The people who live with them.

Not an institution that turns care into permission.

### “Someone has to ration scarce resources.”

Then everyone sees the stores.

Everyone sees the numbers.

Nobody gets a private warehouse.

### “Someone has to stand watch.”

Then the watch rotates.

A permanent officer class is not required to stay awake at night.

### “Someone has to make hard decisions.”

That is exactly the sentence Bastion distrusts.

Because historically, “someone has to make the hard decisions” becomes the justification for making decisions **for everyone else**.

## Bastion Critique of Peacekeepers
^bastion-critique-of-pk

The Bastion challenge is not that order is always bad.

It is that every centralized structure begins by claiming necessity.

Then necessity becomes authority.

Authority becomes entitlement.

Entitlement becomes permanence.

Their fear is **capture**.

They look at Wards Point and ask:

- who decides who gets access?
- who controls the books?
- who controls the food?
- who decides what is “strategic”?
- who audits the people doing the rationing?
- what happens when “temporary” restrictions never end?

A useful Bastion accusation is:

**“You think authority is legitimate because you gave it a uniform.”**

## Potential Civilian Benefit
^potential-civilian-benifit

- immediate shelter;
- food production;
- community space;
- cultural and educational continuity;
- broader community access;
- visible and shared resource use;
- local autonomy;
- reduced dependence on institutions that have repeatedly failed.

## Anarchist Weakness
^bastion-weakness

Bastion's system works best when:

- the group is relatively small;
- trust is visible;
- contribution is observable;
- resource use is transparent;
- social consequences matter;
- people know one another.

It becomes harder as success attracts more people.

Potential failure modes include:

- free riders;
- conflicting priorities;
- informal cliques;
- charisma replacing formal authority;
- inconsistent enforcement;
- resource disputes;
- reluctance to make unpopular decisions;
- hidden hierarchy emerging without accountability;
- technical archives being neglected;
- knowledge being lost through necessity or disorder;
- infrastructure knowledge never becoming coordinated city capability.

Their anti-authoritarianism does not eliminate power.

It can simply make power harder to identify.

## Potential Future State
^potential-bastion-future-state

- communal living space;
- future food production in the adaptable atrium;
- shelter for families;
- informal cultural and educational use;
- community access;
- transparent/shared stores;
- rotating responsibilities;
- looser hierarchy;
- less systematic technical preservation;
- greater risk of knowledge loss or inconsistent long-term coordination.

# Ideological Conflict
^ideologicalconflict

The conflict should not be reduced to:

**authoritarian vs anarchist**

The stronger formulation is:

## Peacekeeper Fear

**Chaos abandons the weak.**

## Bastion Fear

**Concentrated authority eventually owns the weak.**

Peacekeepers fear disorder.

Bastion fears capture.

Both fears are supported by the world around them.

The library is therefore a perfect ideological object.

The Peacekeepers see:

**a place where knowledge can be preserved, organized and converted into public capacity.**

Bastion sees:

**a place where knowledge can remain accessible instead of becoming another controlled resource.**

Neither side is arguing from stupidity.

# Character A — Initial Quest Giver
^character-a

The briefer is the person within the briefing faction who has the strongest established relationship with Eli and who is sufficiently embedded in that faction’s decision loop to authorize the Wards Point operation.

Functional definition:

- belongs to the initial claimant selected by [[WPL_INITIAL_CLAIMANT]] when the quest becomes eligible, including its PK tie/fallback rule;
- trusts Eli enough to use him as an independent actor;
- needs the library investigated without committing faction manpower;
- presents that faction's intended use of Wards Point as reasonable and beneficial;
- gives the operational instruction to enter, secure the main entrance, clear the building and report back.

## If Peacekeeper-Aligned

The character should present duty before authority.

Possible attitude:

- command structures are failing;
- someone still has to protect people;
- local initiative is increasingly necessary;
- order must be rebuilt from the ground up;
- Strongly influenced by Major Matt's systems view for the future;
- Major Matt or other officers may be cited as examples of people who understand field reality.

The character should not talk as though the Peacekeepers formally exist yet.

## If Bastion-Aligned

The briefer is the person within the briefing faction who has the strongest established relationship with Eli and who is sufficiently embedded in that faction’s decision loop to authorize the Wards Point operation.

The character should present autonomy as a learned response to betrayal, not adolescent rebellion.

Possible attitude:

- institutions promised protection and failed;
- transparency matters because centralized control has repeatedly hidden failure;
- people can cooperate without surrendering permanent authority;
- Wards Point should belong to the people who use it, not to another institution.

Questions to answer later:

- why does this person personally trust Eli?
- why can they authorize or arrange the reward?
- what do they omit from the initial framing?
- how does the tone change at different trust levels?

# Character B — Competing Radio Contact
^character-b

Functional definition:

- represents the rival claimant;
- knows Eli entered because the rival faction has Wards Point under observation;
- already has a plausible way to reach Eli by radio;
- delivers the compressed competing claim in [[WPL_03_COMPETING_CLAIM]] after [[WPL_EVENT02_DOOR]] secures the main entrance;
- approaches Eli after the other faction supplied the initial brief; that selection may reflect higher trust, a PK tie, or the PK fallback under [[WPL_INITIAL_CLAIMANT]];
- asks Eli to hear them out before he reports success.

## Dramatic Function

This character does not merely deliver the opposite faction's sales pitch.

They challenge the initial framing.

Their job is to make Eli reconsider the assumptions carried into the library.

Questions to answer later:

- how was Eli's radio channel/frequency established?
- what campaign-specific personal history supports the established radio relationship?

The authored surveillance admission and counter-case are in [[WPL_03_COMPETING_CLAIM]]. Evidence-informed follow-up uses the relevant counter-claimant variant in [[WPL_06_ATRIUM]].

# Additional Character Possibilities
^additional-characters

Do not create these merely because the design has room for them.

Possible functions:

- neutral/local civilian;
- library survivor/caretaker;
- parent or elder who complicates the “fit and able” survival model;
- civilian voice representing people who will live with Eli's decision rather than a faction that benefits from it.

Add only if they perform a necessary evidentiary or dramatic function.

# Character Doctrine
^character-doctrine

Character biography and final campaign casting remain production dependencies. The current mission function, faction arguments and dialogue are established by the authored beats; see [[Design_Doctrine]] and [[06_Dialogue_Working]].

Do not invent protagonist motivation that conflicts with the production character model.

Do not make either faction fanatical simply to simplify the moral choice.

The player should be able to understand why reasonable people would choose either side.

The later authoritarianism of the Peacekeepers should grow from a real problem.

The anti-authoritarianism of Bastion should grow from observed institutional betrayal.

The mission works only if both positions remain credible long enough for the player to hesitate before reporting Wards Point secured.
